🏛️ The Real Times Square: A Field Guide

Until 1904, the bowtie intersection where Broadway slashes diagonally across Seventh Avenue in Manhattan was a gritty carriage-and-horse district called Longacre Square, dominated by harness shops and William H. Vanderbilt's American Horse Exchange. Everything changed when publisher Adolph Ochs moved the headquarters of The New York Times into a narrow twenty-five-story tower at Forty-Second Street and persuaded Mayor George B. McClellan Jr. to rename the plaza and its new subway station Times Square. By the 1920s, zoning rules actually required buildings in the district to maintain massive illuminated signage to keep the theatrical energy alive, a mandate that survived the sleazy grindhouse cinema decades of the 1970s to become the hyper-commercial canyon of high-definition LED billboards we know today.

Standing in the middle of Father Duffy Square in the twenty-first century feels like climbing inside a giant pinball machine during a blackout testing drill. Even at three in the morning, fifty-story-tall digital screens blast advertisements for prescription pharmaceuticals, fast fashion, and Broadway musicals at eyeball-searing brightness while steam hisses up through orange plastic Con Edison chimneys in the asphalt. On the pavement below, three hundred thousand daily pedestrians weave around unlicensed costumed characters in slightly mildewed Spider-Man and Elmo suits who aggressively demand twenty-dollar tips the second a confused tourist raises a smartphone camera.

Into this sensory blender stepped @StanzaSlammer, who wrote "Utterly lacking in poetic justice!" to complain that "Rhymes Square" offered "not a single rhyming couplet" on any of its flashing screens. Traveling to Forty-Second Street expecting a quiet outdoor literary salon where billboards project iambic pentameter is like showing up at the Super Bowl hoping for a harp recital. While Tin Pan Alley composers certainly cranked out plenty of rhyming lyrics a few blocks south in the early nineteen-hundreds, the only verses you will hear in Times Square today involve a guy in a knockoff Batman cape demanding cash or threatening to delete your vacation photo.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Keep walking if anyone tries to hand you a 'free' autographed CD or offers a photo with a cartoon character, and use the red TKTS glass steps at the north end for discounted same-day Broadway tickets.
