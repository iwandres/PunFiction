🏛️ The Real Burj Khalifa: A Field Guide

Designed by Chicago skyscraper veteran Adrian Smith of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill using a three-lobed buttressed footprint inspired by the desert spider lily, the Burj Khalifa tops out at 828 meters and 163 habitable floors, more than twice the height of the Empire State Building. Known as the Burj Dubai during its six-year construction, the tower was abruptly renamed on opening night in January 2010 to honor Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan, ruler of neighboring Abu Dhabi, who had discreetly wired ten billion dollars days earlier to bail out Dubai World's debt crisis. Its exterior is clad in 26,000 hand-cut glass panels that take thirty-six window washers on mechanized cradles three full months to squeegee from top to bottom.

You cannot simply walk through a front door off the street; reaching the 'At the Top' elevators requires hiking a mile through the subterranean levels of the Dubai Mall past an indoor shark aquarium, an Olympic ice rink, and four hundred luxury handbag boutiques. Even with a pre-booked sixty-dollar ticket for the 124th floor, you will spend up to an hour snaking through velvet ropes before a double-deck elevator shoots you skyward at ten meters per second while projections of other buildings you are passing flash on the walls. Stepping onto the outdoor terrace at 452 meters, you peer through narrow vertical slots in the glass wall into a hundred-degree desert haze that makes the Persian Gulf look like warm milk.

All that structural engineering left an empty stomach for @GreasyGastronaut, who rode the elevator 124 floors into the Arabian sky expecting the Burger Khalifa. In 'More Grease Than Glamour!', our fast-food pilgrim complained that their 'towering desert dream crumbled like stale sesame buns' and expressed shock that the observation deck wasn't slathered in secret special sauce or stacked with charbroiled beef patties. Paying fifty-five dirhams at the summit cafe for a cappuccino dusted with twenty-four-karat edible gold leaf is undeniably steep, but blaming the world's tallest freestanding structure for not coming with crinkle-cut fries and a pickle spear is a high-altitude misunderstanding.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Instead of paying extra for the crowded 148th-floor observation deck, book a table with a window minimum spend at Atmosphere on the 122nd floor for roughly the same price and an actual drink in your hand.
