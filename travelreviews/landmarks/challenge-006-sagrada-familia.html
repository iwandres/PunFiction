🏛️ The Real Sagrada Família: A Field Guide

Work on the Sagrada Família began in March 1882 under diocesan architect Francisco de Paula del Villar, who quit after twelve months and handed the blueprints to a thirty-one-year-old Antoni Gaudí. Gaudí scrapped the neo-Gothic plans and spent forty-three years inventing a forest of leaning stone columns modelled by hanging canvas sacks of birdshot from strings in his studio. By 1925 he had moved his bed into the site workshop, emerging in June 1926 only to step in front of the Number 30 tram; because his coat was held together with safety pins and he carried no wallet, cabbies mistook the genius for a beggar. The basilica finally obtained its first legal building permit from Barcelona City Council in 2019, after 137 years of unlicensed masonry.

Approaching the Nativity facade today means stepping onto a four-foot-wide Eixample sidewalk where two thousand people are simultaneously backing into traffic with selfie sticks while staring past six giant yellow tower cranes. Spontaneous visits are impossible: you must reserve a fifteen-minute entry slot online weeks in advance, download an app, and pass through airport X-ray scanners before entering the stained-glass nave. Those who pay extra to ride the tiny elevator up the Passion tower are rewarded with a narrow stone spiral staircase descent of four hundred steps that curls tightly downward like a vertical snail shell, offering dizzy tourists an intimate view of the backpack directly in front of their nose.

All that Catalan Modernisme was completely wasted on @StickyTourist, who strolled up Carrer de Mallorca with a reusable plastic straw thirsty for the Sangria Família. Expecting a festive punchbowl of Rioja, brandy, and diced Valencia oranges, our reviewer left one star ('Sticky Situation, Unholy Mess!') after finding zero fruit cocktails inside a Roman Catholic minor basilica. One can only imagine the crushing disappointment of standing beneath a sixty-meter limestone vault designed to represent the New Jerusalem while complaining to a bewildered sacristan that nobody has brought out a frosted glass pitcher, ice cubes, or a wooden spoon to stir the peaches.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Book a late afternoon ticket around 4:00 PM so the low western sun hits the orange and red stained glass on the Passion side, turning the entire white stone interior into a glowing furnace.
