🏛️ The Real Tower Bridge: A Field Guide

Opened in June 1894 after eight years of work by city architect Sir Horace Jones and engineer Sir John Wolfe Barry, Tower Bridge looks medieval only because Queen Victoria and Parliament insisted its 11,000-ton steel skeleton be dressed up in Cornish granite and Portland stone to match the neighboring Tower of London. Its twin thousand-ton bascules ('seesaws' in French) originally pivoted upward in ninety seconds flat using giant steam-driven hydraulic accumulators housed in the southern pier. In December 1952, the bridge watchman failed to spot the Number 78 double-decker bus in a thick London pea-souper and began raising the roadway while Albert Gunter was mid-crossing; Gunter floored the accelerator, jumped his bus across a three-foot gap onto the rising north span, and was awarded a ten-pound bonus by London Transport.

Roughly forty percent of tourists standing on the walkways still congratulate themselves on visiting 'London Bridge,' completely unaware that the actual London Bridge is a drab 1973 concrete slab half a mile upstream. If you pay thirteen pounds forty to ride the elevator up the north tower into the high-level pedestrian walkways, you can walk across an eleven-meter glass floor installed forty-two meters above the Thames while watching red buses and commuter ferries pass directly beneath your shoes. Inevitably, a small queue forms at the glass edge as parents try to coax terrified toddlers and stubborn golden retrievers across the transparent panels while someone's wet umbrella drips steadily onto the glass.

Dripping umbrellas were the only moisture available to @DrippingTourist, who strolled onto the Thames embankment with a loofah and a bar of Imperial Leather expecting the Shower Bridge. Complaining in 'Definitely a wash-out experience!' that the Gothic Revival towers dispensed 'no soap' and failed to deliver a proper hot rinse, our reviewer mistook a sideways November drizzle in Southwark for defective bathroom plumbing. While the original Victorian steam boilers under the south bank pumped thousands of gallons of pressurized water to lift the bascules, expecting Horace Jones's granite turrets to shampoo your hair in the middle of central London traffic is a bold hygiene strategy.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Check the bridge lift schedule on the official Tower Bridge website a day ahead so you can watch the giant bascules swing open from the south bank outside City Hall for free.
