🏛️ The Real Pyramids of Giza: A Field Guide

Finished around 2560 BC for the Fourth Dynasty Pharaoh Khufu, the Great Pyramid at Giza held the record as the tallest structure on Earth for 3,800 years, losing the title only when Lincoln Cathedral raised a precarious timber spire in 1311. Its 2.3 million limestone and granite blocks average two and a half tons each and were originally sheathed in polished white Tura limestone until earthquakes and medieval builders stripped the casing for Cairo mosques. In 1196, Sultan Al-Aziz Uthman actually hired a crew to demolish the pyramids; after eight gruelling months of prying stones off the smaller Pyramid of Menkaure with levers, his workmen left one vertical gash on the north face and quit, concluding that unbuilding a mountain was just as ruinous as building one.

Clever camera angles pointing west into the Sahara always conceal the fact that the Giza plateau begins roughly two hundred yards from a two-story building housing both a Pizza Hut and a Kentucky Fried Chicken. Instead of solitary desert contemplation, the modern visitor navigates a gauntlet of men waving keffiyeh scarves who promise that a camel ride costs five dollars, only to clarify mid-desert that getting off the camel costs an additional fifty. If you buy the supplemental ticket to crawl inside Khufu's Grand Gallery, you will duck-walk up a four-foot wooden chute at a forty-degree incline in stagnant, ninety-five-degree air that smells of ancient dust and forty damp tourists from Frankfurt, arriving at an empty granite room with no air conditioning.

Still, an empty granite sarcophagus was the least of the problems for @CrankyElder, who booked a flight to Cairo expecting the Pyramids of Geezer. In a scathing review headlined 'Ancient, dusty, and full of complaints!', our correspondent expressed dismay that the Old Kingdom monuments were made of silent nummulitic limestone rather than irritable retirees searching for misplaced dentures and complaining about their sciatica. While the pharaohs certainly planned to spend eternity on the desert plateau, they did so as royal mummies wrapped in linen rather than cranky pensioners in beige cardigans arguing over the thermostat in the community recreation lounge.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Politely ignore anyone at the gate claiming to be an official ticket inspector who needs to walk you to a camel stable, and grab lunch on the rooftop terrace above the KFC across from the Sphinx for the best view without the hassle.
