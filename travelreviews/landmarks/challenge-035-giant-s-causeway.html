🏛️ The Real Giant's Causeway: A Field Guide

Between fifty and sixty million years ago, as the North American tectonic plate ripped away from Eurasia to open the North Atlantic, torrents of molten tholeiitic basalt flooded across County Antrim and cooled in a deep river valley. As the lava shrank, physics did what physics does best and cracked the rock into roughly 40,000 interlocking vertical columns, mostly neat six-sided polygons alongside the occasional rebel pentagon or octagon. Unsatisfied with thermal contraction as a narrative hook, early Irish storytellers credited the whole thing to the warrior giant Fionn mac Cumhaill, who allegedly laid the stones to march across the sea and thrash the Scottish giant Benandonner before chickening out and hiding in a giant baby cradle.

Visiting the Causeway today begins with a minor test of Irish reading comprehension at the sleek, grass-roofed Visitor Experience building up on the cliff ridge. The National Trust charges around fifteen pounds for entry to the exhibition hall and main car park, though the actual coast path down to the rocks is a public right-of-way and technically completely free if you arrive on foot or regional bus. Down at the shoreline, four hundred people in bright yellow rain ponchos hop precariously across slick wet basalt stepping stones like contestants on an uninsured game show, while whistle-blowing wardens shout through megaphones every time someone wanders too close to a ten-foot Atlantic swell.

This geological spectacle proved far too stationary for @ImpatientGoliath, who dropped a one-star review titled 'The Longest Stop-Over!' and grumbled about 'endless hexagonal boredom' because the stones refused to move. It takes a remarkably literal mind to read the word 'Causeway' on a map of Northern Ireland, drive all the way past Bushmills, and stand on Paleogene volcanic rock waiting for it to operate like an airport moving walkway. Blaming Finn McCool for dawdling when the basalt has been sitting quietly in the exact same spot since the Paleocene epoch seems slightly unfair to the giant, who at least had the excuse of pretending to be an infant.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Skip the paid shuttle bus down the hill unless your knees are on strike; the twenty-minute Shepherd's Steps loop offers a much better view from above before you scramble onto the stones.
