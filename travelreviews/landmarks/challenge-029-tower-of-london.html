🏛️ The Real Tower of London: A Field Guide

Founded by William the Conqueror in late 1066 to intimidate rebellious Londoners, the Tower of London centers on the White Tower, a twenty-seven-meter limestone keep imported stone by stone from Caen in Normandy and whitewashed by Henry III in 1240. Over nine centuries, the eighteen-acre complex has simultaneously functioned as a royal palace, an execution ground for two of Henry VIII's wives, the Royal Mint, a public records office, and a chaotic royal menagerie where thirteenth-century Londoners could watch King Haakon IV of Norway's gift polar bear swim in the Thames on a long leather leash to catch salmon. By decree of Charles II, at least six captive ravens with one wing slightly trimmed are kept on the green at all times lest the kingdom and the fortress fall.

Visiting today costs thirty-four pounds eighty per adult, a sum that entitles you to stand in a drizzling queue outside the Jewel House before stepping onto a slow-moving airport conveyor belt that slides you past the Imperial State Crown at two miles per hour so nobody can linger over the 530-carat Cullinan I diamond. Out on Tower Green, thirty-seven Yeoman Warders (never call them Beefeaters if you want a polite answer, though they will tolerate it for a photo) deliver booming forty-five-minute comedy routines about Tudor decapitations while wearing wool tunics that cost seven thousand pounds apiece. Meanwhile, the resident ravens patrol the benches beside the café, waiting to mug a distracted child for a sausage roll.

Medieval stone battlements and corvid kleptomania offered scant comfort to @SoggyBrit, who stepped out of Tower Hill Underground station in a downpour expecting the Towel of London. Writing under the headline 'More Like Drowner of London!', our damp reviewer complained that the eleventh-century castle was 'thin, scratchy... definitely not fluffy enough for royalty' and completely useless for drying off after a swim, adding that the ravens kept trying to peck it. While fifteen-foot-thick ragstone ramparts have kept out rebel armies since 1078, rubbing your wet ears against the Traitor's Gate portcullis and expecting four-hundred-gram Egyptian terrycloth is a distinctly soggy mistake.

💡 Practical Visitor Tip: Walk straight to the Jewel House the second the gates open at 9:00 AM before the conveyor-belt queue builds to an hour, then double back to the main entrance for the 10:00 AM Yeoman Warder tour.
