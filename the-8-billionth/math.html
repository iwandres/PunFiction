01 // MULTIPLYING ODDS & SHANNON BITS

Why Ordinary Facts Add Up—and What a “Shannon Bit” Means

You don’t need a one-in-a-million superpower to be one-of-one. When two facts have nothing to do with each other—say, being left-handed (about 1 in 10 people) and being born in March (about 1 in 12 people)—you multiply the fractions together: only 1 in 120 people match both. Stack five or six everyday traits from different parts of your life, and 8.3 billion drops to single digits.

Why measure progress in Shannon bits? If we used a normal 0–100% progress bar, your very first click could jump the bar to 88% full while you still had nearly a billion people left—and shrinking from a million people down to 1 wouldn’t visibly move the bar at all! Instead, our meter uses Shannon bits—named after mathematician Claude Shannon, who coined the word bit to count how many times a clue cuts a group in half.

The rule is simple: 1 Shannon bit = cutting the remaining crowd in half once (one 50/50 coin flip, or one crisp Guess Who? question that eliminates half the board). Turning fractions into halvings lets every trait add steady points onto the bar:

1 in 2 = +1 bit  ·  1 in 8 = +3 bits  ·  1 in 1,000 ≈ +10 bits  ·  1 in 8.3B = 32.95 bits
Cut 8,300,000,000 in half about 33 times in a row (2 × 2 × … × 2 thirty-three times ≈ 8.59 billion) and only 1 person is left standing.
02 // THE VENN DIAGRAM RULE

Why We Don’t Let Overlapping Quirks Double-Count

Multiplying fractions (or adding full Shannon bits) only plays fair when two facts are truly independent. In real life, plenty of habits, places, and hobbies travel in packs. If you bake sourdough bread, you’re already much more likely than a random person on Earth to own vinyl records or weigh coffee beans. So before awarding bits, our math checks for three kinds of overlap:

1 · ONE INSIDE ANOTHER

Automatic Freebies

If you pick “I ran a marathon” and “I exercise weekly,” we don’t count both—running 26.2 miles already proves you work out! Same for living in Japan and living on an island. Only the narrower fact counts.

2 · PLACE & LANGUAGE

Real Local Percentages

Only ~1.5% of Earth speaks Japanese—unless you live in Japan, where 98.5% of people do. Conversely, 15% of Earth speaks Mandarin, vs. only 1.1% in the US. Clicking in either order lands on the exact same real-world headcount.

3 · SHARED VENN CIRCLES & STARTING FILTERS

Softening Repeat Lifestyle Cuts

Roughly 3 out of 4 people on Earth live in Asia or Africa, 1 in 4 are kids under 15, and most people don’t have a suburban garage or backyard. If you stack four hobbies that all quietly rely on having a yard, spare income, or living in the West—or two hobbies from the exact same personality bubble (like sourdough + vinyl records)—you only earn full bits for that shared filter once. Repeat traits in the same Venn circle earn fewer bits, and we’ll show you the adjusted odds right on screen.

03 // VISUALIZATION STRATEGY

How We Fit 8.3 Billion People on One Screen

Even a sharp laptop or phone screen only has a few million pixels—nowhere near enough to draw 8.3 billion people at once. So the crowd canvas works like a 5-step zoom lens, stepping closer every time your remaining group crosses a new scale:

1 dot = 1M  →  1 dot = 1,000  →  1 dot = 1 person  →  silhouettes  →  You (1 of 1)
Keeps between 1 and 9,999 visible dots on screen at all times so you can actually watch the crowd shrink.
  • Sunflower Spiral Packing: Instead of stiff rows and columns, the dots arrange themselves in the exact same natural spiral a sunflower uses to pack its seeds—keeping the crowd evenly spaced with zero awkward grid gaps.
  • Life-Sized Scale Outlines: Behind the dots, we sketch a real-world place or vehicle built for that exact crowd size—from a spinning Earth down to a stadium, jumbo jet, school bus, or elevator—so big numbers instantly feel human-sized.
04 // DATA SOURCES & CAVEATS

Where Do Our Numbers Come From?

Not every trait on Earth is measured the same way, so our stats blend hard census data with smart ballpark estimates where no official count exists:

  • Countries, Languages & Biology (Hard Data): National populations, age groups, spoken languages, blood types, adult height, and major genetic traits come from real demographic and scientific benchmarks—including UN population reports, national censuses, language surveys, and medical studies.
  • Hobbies, Sports & Daily Quirks (Educated Estimates): No government census knocks on doors asking who keeps a sourdough starter or eats kiwifruit with the skin on! For everyday habits and hobbies, we start from real-world clues—like scuba certifications, pilot licenses, sports federations, or survey polls—and scale them realistically across all 8.3 billion people (including kids and non-Western countries).
  • Custom Typed Facts (Two-Step Ballparks): Anything you type from scratch is estimated in two steps—finding the size of the broader global group first, then taking a realistic slice—and saved so everyone gets the exact same number. Treat hobby and quirk counts as well-grounded ballpark figures rather than exact headcounts!

01 // INFORMATION THEORY

Why We Measure Progress in Shannon Bits

A linear progress bar fails immediately at planetary scale. Selecting a single common trait like “I play volleyball” (p = 0.12) eliminates 88% of humanity on click one—leaving the remaining 996,000,000 people squished into the final 12% of a linear bar for the rest of the session.

Instead, the Uniqueness Meter operates in Claude Shannon’s self-information (surprisal) space. Isolating a single individual from N0 = 8.3 × 109 equiprobable humans requires accumulating:

Hmax = log2(8.3 × 109) ≈ 32.95017 bits
Every 50/50 coin-flip trait contributes 1.00 bit; every 10× order-of-magnitude reduction contributes log2(10) ≈ 3.322 bits.

After stacking k active traits with effective conditional survival rates peff(1), …, peff(k), the unrounded expected headcount Ek and cumulative surprisal Ik satisfy:

Ek = N0 · ∏i=1..k peff(i),      Ik = ∑i=1..k −log2 peff(i) = log2(N0 / Ek)
02 // JOINT PROBABILITY & CORRELATION

Defeating Naive Independence: P(A ∩ B) ≠ P(A) · P(B)

If human traits were statistically independent Bernoulli variables, narrowing humanity would just multiply unconditional global prevalences pbase(i). In reality, human behaviors cluster around shared latent factors—geography, climate, fitness, income, sensory genetics, and subculture. By the chain rule of probability:

P(T1 ∩ T2 ∩ … ∩ Tk) = P(T1) · P(T2 | T1) · … · P(Tk | T1, …, Tk−1)

Whenever two traits have positive covariance—say, “I live above 5,000 ft” (5.8%) and “I backcountry ski” (0.045%)—the conditional probability P(T2 | T1) is substantially larger than the unconditional marginal P(T2). Treating them as independent would hallucinate uniqueness orders of magnitude too quickly.

Because we cannot store a dense 6,000 × 6,000 empirical covariance matrix—especially when users can type arbitrary free-text traits—our engine evaluates the conditional chain through five hierarchical rules that preserve exact click-order symmetry and Kolmogorov’s Fréchet bounds:

RULE A · P(A | B) = 1

Transitive Subset & AI Subsumption

Transitively closed parent–child links (C ⊂ B ⊂ A) eliminate redundant ancestors across curated, parametric, and AI traits. Selecting Ultramarathoner supersedes both Regular runner and Exercises weekly; Born on Feb 29 supersedes February birthday; Lives in Japan supersedes Island resident; and 2+ spoken languages supersede Bilingual (2+) (peff = 1.0, 0 bits).

RULE B · P(A ∩ B) = 0

Partitions & Contradictions

Single-valued partitions (ABO/Rh blood type, country of residence, stature bracket, eye color, birth month, birth decade) and symmetric contradiction graphs (Drinks coffee vs. Zero caffeine, Vegetarian vs. Pescatarian, Only child vs. Youngest/Oldest sibling, Never broken a bone vs. 3+ broken bones) cleanly replace incompatible prior selections.

RULE C · EXACT BAYES CONDITIONING

Bidirectional Country–Language & Geographic Coupling

Lifestyle tags alone miss sharp geographic couplings—both positive (only 1.55% of Earth speaks Japanese, yet ~98.5% of residents in Japan do) and negative (15.0% of Earth speaks Mandarin, yet only ~1.1% of US residents do). Country tables store empirical conditional rates r = P(L | C) for spoken languages, island geography, Southern Hemisphere, and high elevation, calibrated against the Total Law of Probability (∑C P(C) · r < P(L)) and evaluated symmetrically in any selection order via Bayes’ theorem:

P(L | C) = r,      P(C | L1, …, Ls) = min(0.995,  P(C) · ∏j rj / P(L1 ∩ … ∩ Ls))

Result: Lives in Japan ↔ Speaks Japanese converges to ~121.0M humans in either click order (instead of a 65× drop), while Lives in the US ↔ Speaks Mandarin converges to ~3.79M Americans (instead of assuming 15% of the US speaks Mandarin).

RULE D · SYMMETRIC PARTIAL OVERLAP (γ = 0.65)

Bayes-Symmetric Power-Law Dampening Across a 40-Tag Latent Space

Every lifestyle trait—curated, parametric, and Gemini-estimated—projects onto 1 to 4 tags in a closed 40-tag latent taxonomy 𝒞: 36 topical domains plus 4 cross-topic psychographic bridges (quant_systemizer, tactile_analog_ritual, gear_optimizer, and thrill_risk), while unlinked Mendelian loci (handedness, twinning, colorblindness) and pure demographic partitions stay orthogonal. If active trait i (with base surprisal bi = −log2 pbase(i)) overlaps mi prior traits sorted descending by base bits b(1) ≥ … ≥ b(mi), its marginal bits follow the Bayes-symmetric γ = 0.65 curve:

ΔIi = bi − ∑r=1..mi γr−1(1 − γ) · min(bi,  b(r)),     peff(i) = 2−ΔIi

Why cap each overlap term at min(bi, b(r))? By Shannon’s mutual-information bound, a broad event with b(r) bits cannot explain more than (1 − γ) b(r) bits of a rarer event. When bi ≤ b(mi), the sum telescopes identically to peff = pbaseγm (ΔIi = γm bi: 100%, 65%, 42.25%, 27.46% of bits). When a rare trait follows a broad trait (bi > b(r)), bounding shared bits by min(bi, b(r)) yields the exact Bayes dual P(B | A) = P(B) · P(A)γ−1—guaranteeing exact k!-permutation invariance and Kolmogorov’s Fréchet bound P(A ∩ B) ≤ min(P(A), P(B)) across all 105,950 trait pairs.

RULE E · PAY-THE-GATE-ONCE (α = 0.50)

De-Correlating Hidden Demographic Macro-Gates

Roughly 75% of humanity lives in Asia and Africa, 26% are under age 15, and most humans live in apartments or dense towns without garages or yards. Without macro-denominator gating, stacking sourdough starter, 50+ vinyl records, blue eyes, and backyard weather station would repeatedly re-eliminate global infants, non-Western populations, and apartment dwellers on every click. Each trait therefore declares its membership in 4 closed baseGates—adult_15plus (P = 0.74, 0.43b), connected_urban_affluent (P = 0.22, 2.18b), euro_western_skew (P = 0.16, 2.64b), and yard_homeowner_space (P = 0.14, 2.84b). Subsequent traits sharing an already-paid gate deduct min(ci, maxj<i cj) where c = α · (−log2 Pgate) (α = 0.5, proportionally capped so cluster + gate deductions always preserve positive marginal bits and full click-order symmetry), while Layer-3 Gemini estimation stays 100% stateless.

04 // DISCRETE STOPPING THRESHOLD & POISSON CLONES

The Ek ≤ 1.49 Threshold (and Does “1” Guarantee Zero Clones?)

Standard integer rounding maps 1.49 down to 1, while naive truncation or loose rounding would let someone at Ek = 1.9 claim victory early. Our engine enforces a strict piecewise boundary:

Ndisplay = 1  if Ek ≤ 1.49,    else max(2, round(Ek))

A subtle distinction for probability purists: Because you exist and conditioned the query on your own traits, you contribute a deterministic 1 to the global count. Among the remaining N0 − 1 strangers on Earth, the number of statistical clones Kothers follows a rare-event Binomial distribution well-approximated by Poisson(λ = Ek):

  • At Ek = 1.00 (32.95 bits): the expected number of strangers matching your list is λ = 1, giving P(Kothers = 0) = e−1 ≈ 36.8% chance of zero clones on Earth (and a 73.6% chance of at most one clone).
  • At Ek = 0.10 (36.27 bits): P(Kothers = 0) = e−0.1 ≈ 90.5% confidence that not a single other human alive shares your stack.
  • At Ek ≤ 0.01 (≥ 39.6 bits, our “Timeline Eraser” badge): P(Kothers = 0) ≥ 99.0%.
05 // BAYESIAN FERMI PRIORS & GUARDRAILS

How Custom Free-Text Traits Are Estimated Without Western Bias

When you type an unscripted trait into the omnibox (e.g., “I grow tropical pitcher plants indoors”), a structured-output LLM resolver performs a two-stage Fermi decomposition anchored to global UN demographic baselines—explicitly accounting for age distribution (infants through elderly) and global regional shares (Asia ~60%, Africa ~18%, Americas ~13%, Europe ~9%):

N̂trait = Nbase pool × P(quirk | base pool),      pbase = clamp(N̂trait / 8.3×109, 10−7, 0.999)
  • Universal Baseline & Tautology Guardrail (≥ 99.9% ≈ 8.29B+ humans): Sample-space constants shared by virtually the entire human pool—such as “I am a human,” “I was born on Earth,” “I have human DNA,” or trivial negations like “I have never walked on the Moon” (< 0.0015 bits)—bounce at the omnibox with zero funnel impact rather than artificially shaving the population.
  • Hard Prevalence Floor (10−7 ≈ 830 humans): Custom traits are server-clamped at pbase ≥ 10−7 unless flagged as an established extreme medical or historical cohort (e.g., lightning-strike survivor at ~3 × 10−6) behind an explicit confirmation step.
  • Anti-Smuggling Compound Splits: Multi-clause inputs (“I’m a left-handed indie game dev who scuba dives”) are intercepted and split into atomic statements so each clause undergoes independent validation and γ = 0.65 tag-overlap checks.
  • Closed-Schema Tagging & Stateless Cache Flywheel: Custom estimates emit 1–4 tags from the exact same 40-tag enum plus 0–4 macro baseGates, evaluated 100% statelessly (never conditioned on a caller’s active stack) and persisted by normalized SHA-256 hash so identical traits resolve globally with deterministic consistency.
06 // VISUALIZATION GEOMETRY

Rendering 8.3 Billion People on a 2D Canvas

Drawing 8.3 × 109 pixels is impossible on a screen with ~2 million pixels. The visualization uses a 5-tier Powers-of-Ten camera (106 → 103 → 1 → concentric silhouettes → spotlight) keeping active particle counts between 1 and 9,999.

To pack thousands of dots into a circular field with uniform area density and zero grid Moiré artifacts, surviving particles n ∈ [0, N − 1] in Tiers 1–3 are arranged along Vogel’s golden-angle phyllotaxis spiral (the same packing geometry found in sunflower seed heads), transitioning in Tier 4 (2 ≤ N ≤ 249) to concentric amphitheater rings with capacity 6r per ring:

θn = n · π(3 − √5) ≈ n · 137.508°,      rn = Renv · √((n + 0.5) / N)

Beneath the particle cluster, 82 architectural, vessel, and cartographic scale outlines span 27 logarithmic population bands—from a rotating orthographic Earth globe at 8.3B down through stadiums, concert halls, jumbo jets, school buses, and elevators to the 1-of-1 spotlight—paired 1-to-1 with the narrowing funnel headline while global trait base headcounts sit isolated in a dedicated Worldwide Fun Fact card.

07 // DATA SOURCES & PRECISION TIERS

Empirical Benchmarks vs. Calibrated Fermi Priors

No live planetary registry tracks all human behaviors simultaneously. Across our static seed tables and custom estimator, trait prevalences fall into four explicit precision tiers:

  • Tier 1 — Demographic & Geographic Baselines (High Precision): Anchored to UN World Population Prospects (N0 = 8.3 × 109, 197 sovereign/territory shares, age structure P(15+) ≈ 0.74, regional shares ~60% Asia, ~18% Africa, ~13% Americas, ~9% Europe) and national vital-statistics birth-month seasonality.
  • Tier 2 — Linguistic, Anthropometric & Genetic Priors (High–Medium Precision): Derived from SIL Ethnologue fluent L1+L2 totals and national censuses (US ACS, Census of India, Eurobarometer) constrained by ∑C P(C) · P(L | C) < P(L); NCD-RisC global adult stature distributions; population-weighted ABO/Rh blood tables; Tremblay et al. (PNAS 2021) DEM human elevation demography (5.8% at ≥ 1,500 m); and meta-analytic genetic/clinical prevalence studies (e.g., Papadatou-Pastou et al. 2020 on left-handedness at 10.6%, Monden et al. 2021 on twinning, WHO surgical volume).
  • Tier 3 — Curated Behavioral & Hobby Synthesis (Order-of-Magnitude Fermi): No global census counts vinyl collectors or sourdough keepers. Where institutional registries exist (PADI certifications, ICAO/FAA pilot licenses, IARU amateur callsigns, SlashData developer surveys, FIDE/online chess pools, FIFA/FIVB participation reports), traits are anchored to registry totals and discounted against full global age, income, and regional denominators.
  • Tier 4 — Unscripted Free-Text Traits (Stateless LLM Fermi): Custom inputs are decomposed by Gemini 3.8 Flash (Nbase pool × P(quirk | base pool)) against UN regional/age priors and built-in calibration anchors, accepted across [10−7, 0.999) (with ≥ 99.9% sample-space tautologies bounced at 0 bits), and cached by SHA-256 hash—designed as internally consistent order-of-magnitude priors rather than literal government headcounts.
